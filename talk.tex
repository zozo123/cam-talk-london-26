An environment is an interaction contract and may span multiple machines or the physical world. A VM can contain an environment, but the VM alone does not specify a task, reward, or termination rule. I use fork broadly for a supported branch of execution; I am not claiming every implementation is POSIX fork or that every resource can be copied.}

% 07
\begin{frame}[t]{What belongs to the state?}
\lead{Reproducing a continuation requires more than a filesystem copy.}
\[
S_t=(F_t,\ M_t,\ H_t,\ R_t\ ;\ X_t)
\]
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{3.5cm}Y@{}}
$F_t$: files and artifacts & Repository, dependencies, fixtures, build outputs.\\
$M_t$: machine state & Processes, memory, supported devices and runtime state.\\
$H_t$: agent history & Messages, tool results, policy identity, pending decisions.\\
$R_t$: randomness & Generator state and declared reseeding policy.\\
$X_t$: external state & Services, clocks, identities, devices, physical systems.\\
\end{tabularx}
\claim{A capture manifest identifies what is copied, reconstructed, or left external.}
\note{[1:00] This is a conceptual state decomposition, not a promise that one snapshot format captures all components. A VM snapshot may preserve process memory yet leave the agent's remote conversation history outside it. A copied random generator creates a coupling until streams diverge or are explicitly reseeded. External service state needs a declared policy: freeze a fixture, replay responses, recreate the service, or accept an uncontrolled dependency. The manifest makes those choices auditable.}
\end{frame}

% 08
\begin{frame}[t]{A continuation contract}
\lead{Restoration should preserve the behavior relevant to the task.}
\[
 d\!\left(\mathcal{L}(O^{\pi}_{\mathrm{restored}}),
           \mathcal{L}(O^{\pi}_{\mathrm{direct}})\right)\leq\varepsilon
\]
\vspace{.15cm}
\begin{itemize}
\item Fix the continuation policy $\pi$, observation boundary $O$, and external-state policy.
\item Choose a task-relevant distance $d$ and tolerance $\varepsilon$.
\item Check declared invariants before measuring search performance.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{.2cm}
\tagline{Example: identical parser fixtures and outputs; bounded timing variation.}
\claim{A successful restore is the beginning of a fidelity test.}
\note{[1:15] This is a proposed observational contract. The law symbol denotes the distribution of observations under the declared continuation, not equality of every machine bit. For deterministic tests we may require exact agreement; stochastic simulations need a justified distributional criterion. Finite tests check specified observations and cannot certify every possible policy or event. Restoration fidelity, candidate quality, and scientific validity are separate axes. We establish the first before attributing an outcome to a changed candidate.}
\end{frame}

\claim{Local rollback cannot rewind a remote service or a wet-lab assay.}
\note{[1:00] A branch in a simulation is a controlled computational alternative conditional on a model. A learned model may predict cheaply but brings approximation error. Neither makes the real world checkpointable. Likewise, restoring an API client does not undo an email, a database write, or a robot movement. External effects require transactions, mocks, compensation where possible, or separate authorization. The scientific question is whether the model and measurement justify the conclusion outside the captured boundary.}

\vspace{.4cm}
\begin{itemize}
\item Source edits must reach the executing program through compilation or reloading.
\item Invalidate artifacts affected by the intervention.
\item Bind the evaluation receipt to the exact resulting artifact.
\end{itemize}
\claim{Only preparation that remains valid can be reused.}
\note{[0:55] A common shortcut in this story is to patch a file and assume a restored process now runs the new implementation. It does not. The candidate has to rebuild, reload, restart, or otherwise change the executing program. Reuse repository preparation and expensive fixtures only where the intervention leaves them valid. Content-addressed build artifacts can be shared with explicit scope and provenance. This lifecycle is also why restore latency alone is a poor measure of useful search cost.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[t]{When does branching pay?}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\column{.55\textwidth}
\small
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{\rm cold}&=N(P+W+V)\\
C_{\rm fork}&=P+H+N(R+D+W+V)
\end{aligned}
\]
\vspace{.15cm}
$P$: reusable preparation; $H$: capture.\\
$R$: restore; $D$: divergence overhead.\\
$W$: candidate work; $V$: evaluation.
\vspace{.2cm}
\claim{$(N-1)P>H+N(R+D)$}
\column{.41\textwidth}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=.47cm,y=.039cm]

\end{tikzpicture}
\tagline{Illustration: $P=10$, $H=2$,\\$R+D=1$, $W+V=2$.}
\end{columns}
\sources{Analytic illustration, not a measured speedup. Add mechanism-specific costs and contention to the experiment.}
\note{[1:20] This additive resource model assumes both paths do the same candidate work and evaluation. \begin{frame}[t]{Stochastic improvement}
\lead{A preserved parent lets a search policy try alternatives and return.}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm,y=1cm]
\node[state,minimum width=2.0cm] (s) at (0,0) {incumbent};
\node[candidate,minimum width=2.6cm] (a) at (4,1) {perturbation A};
\node[candidate,minimum width=2.6cm] (b) at (4,-1) {perturbation B};
\node[evidence,minimum width=2.9cm] (k) at (8.5,1) {compare + retain};
\draw[flow] (s)--(a);\draw[flow] (s)--(b);\draw[flow] (a)--(k);
\draw[message] (b.south) to[bend left=25] node[below,font=\scriptsize]{reject / restore} (s.south);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item Propose a change under a fixed objective and constraints.
\item Compare with appropriate uncertainty; retain the evidence of failure too.

\small Route complementary observations while preserving selection and dependence history.\normalsize

\note{[1:10] These are connected research directions rather than three claims of solved problems. Checkpoint placement is a decision about future work reuse and the distribution of experience. The theoretical challenge is to reason about these decisions together; the systems challenge is to expose sufficient state, cost, and provenance to support that reasoning.}
