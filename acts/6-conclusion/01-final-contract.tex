\qadetail{Forkable execution and adaptive orchestration are distinct layers. The talk motivates their combination; it does not claim to replace stored-program hardware or report a completed general autonomous-computer system. Two research questions: can an ancestry/fixtures/feedback manifest support a calibrated dependence model or a justified refusal of a precision claim? Can restore renew authority and reject stale publication across failure and uncertain external effects? The decisive live-state systems result needs useful reached state that survives capture, remains valid in private continuations, and is cheaper to restore than to reconstruct with a warm alternative. Code edits may require reload or restart. The built reducer handles common-target numerical measurements under declared assumptions; it does not choose or validate patches. Completed incidents do not measure fork-induced correlation. Keep the evidence ledger, exported bytes, exact-artifact verification and current authority distinct.}
\note{[1:00] The story began with state and structure in biological physics, representation and provenance in genomics, and feedback in self-driving systems. Build reuse showed that preparation has a real cost; the factory showed that executing a repair does not finish the job. Forkable execution lets us explore alternatives from a useful state. A useful continuation must return its exact artifact, evidence identities, and authority context. Verification determines what the result establishes, and a controller decides what may continue or be published. That is the systems problem for self-driving computers.}
