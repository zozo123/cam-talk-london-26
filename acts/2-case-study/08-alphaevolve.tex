\qadetail{DeepMind reports that the discovered Borg scheduling heuristic recovered an average 0.7\% of Google's worldwide compute resources, used in production for over a year at announcement. A matrix-multiplication kernel achieved a 23\% kernel speedup and reduced Gemini training time by 1\%. These are distinct workloads and endpoints, not additive gains. Gemini Flash supplies breadth and Gemini Pro supplies depth; algorithm proposals undergo automated validity and performance evaluation and evolutionary selection. The reported gains belong to the discovered algorithms. They are not measurements of a sandbox fork or of our implementation. Candidate execution can be mapped onto continuations, but that mapping is a design connection.}
\note{[1:00] AlphaEvolve makes the search population explicit. A database supplies promising programs; Gemini Flash and Pro propose edits; automated evaluators check validity and performance; evolutionary selection feeds successful programs back. DeepMind reports a scheduling heuristic that recovered point seven percent of worldwide Google compute. On a separate Gemini kernel, a twenty-three percent local speedup became a one percent training-time reduction. That distinction matters for our talk: the result should record the application-level benefit as well as the fast inner operation. The population grows through evaluated programs, not through proposals alone.}
