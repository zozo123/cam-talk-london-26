\qadetail{Dean and Barroso report the 99.9th-percentile latency of a 1,000-key BigTable read, using a hedged request after 10 ms and accepting the first response: 1,800 ms to 74 ms, with 2\% more requests. Their assumption is that the cause of variability does not affect multiple replicas at once. This is a published request-hedging result, not a fork benchmark or agent-correctness measurement. The second request can escape server-specific delay without changing the requested operation. Brown et al., Large Language Monkeys, arXiv:2407.21787: DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct on SWE-bench Lite improves pass-at-least-once coverage from 15.9\% at one sample to 56\% at 250, a 40.1 percentage-point increase. This does not report production selection accuracy. Stroebl, Kapoor and Narayanan, The Limits of Inference Scaling Through Resampling, arXiv:2411.17501: when false-positive acceptance carries negative utility, the optimum is often fewer than ten attempts under their modeled conditions. This is not a universal branch-count recommendation. Neither paper measures our sandbox-fork effect.}

\note{[0:45] Brown and colleagues sampled DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct on SWE-bench Lite. At one sample, a correct patch appeared for fifteen point nine percent of tasks. At 250 samples, coverage reached fifty-six percent. This is pass-at-least-once coverage: some candidate solves the issue. It does not tell us whether a production selector will choose that candidate. The result motivates additional attempts and a concrete decision rule. Autoresearch gives us a simple example of that rule.}
