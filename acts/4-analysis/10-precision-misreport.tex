\tagP\ The controller sets precision ($n\cdot J$) by re-running or re-scoring on held-out data.\par}
\claim{The controller should set precision for each child.}
\sources{Synthetic checks, Eliaz, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09689}{arXiv:2607.09689}, \S4, not agent-task benchmarks.}
\note{[0:55] A receipt can also be false, and isolation cannot prevent it. A copy can claim more certainty than it has. We show two synthetic checks from our preprint. The left panel shows honest shards of unequal size. Information pooling gives a distance of 0.0083 against 0.177 for the equal average. In the right panel, one worker inflates its precision fifty-fold. That worker moves the pooled estimate from about five to seventeen. A heuristic restores it to about five but gives no Byzantine guarantee. The controller should therefore set each child's precision, and children should not report their own.}
