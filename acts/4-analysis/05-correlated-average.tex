\begin{frame}[t]{\ev{\tagO\,\tagA}Correlation raises the variance of an average}
\vspace{0pt}
{\small $Y_i$: one declared outcome of one frozen candidate under a fixed evaluation protocol.\par}
\vspace{.1cm}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\column{.5\textwidth}
{\small Equal marginal variance $\sigma^2$, common pairwise correlation $\rho\geq0$:\par}
\vspace{-.45cm}
\begin{gather*}\operatorname{Var}(\bar Y_N)=\sigma^2\!\left(\rho+\frac{1-\rho}{N}\right)\\[2pt] N_{\rm eff}=\frac{N}{1+(N-1)\rho}\end{gather*}
\vspace{-.02cm}
{\small Variance-equivalent sample size measures precision. Shared bias remains.\par}
\vspace{.1cm}
\noindent\colorbox{Sand}{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep\relax}{\small{\color{Gold}\bfseries\fontsize{7.6}{9}\selectfont ANALOGY}\quad Amdahl's speedup has the same form, $N/(1+(N-1)f)$.}}
\column{.46\textwidth}
{\small\textbf{Examples that assume independent draws}\par}
\vspace{.1cm}
{\small
\begin{ticks}
\item[$\cdot$] Dean \& Barroso: 1 slow request in 100 per server, 100-way fan-out: $1-0.99^{100}\approx63\%$ of requests slow.
\item[$\cdot$] Hypothetical race failing 1 run in 10: 29 green runs give $0.9^{29}=0.047<0.05$.
\item[$\cdot$] Dorfman 1943 pooling. Poolkeh: a model of 295,951 tests for $\sim$9\,M people.
\end{ticks}\par}
\end{columns}
\claim{State the population of draws before counting them as independent.}
\sources{Kish (1965). Amdahl (1967). Dean \& Barroso, CACM 2013. Dorfman (1943). Poolkeh, medRxiv 2020.}
\note{[0:50] Correlation raises the variance of an average. Let Y be one declared outcome of one frozen candidate under a fixed protocol. With common correlation rho, the variance never falls below rho sigma squared. Kish called the ratio a design effect. Amdahl's law has the same form, as an analogy. Dean and Barroso's sixty-three percent, our hypothetical twenty-nine green runs, and pooled testing all assume independent draws. The effective size measures precision only. Shared bias remains. The next slide puts numbers on this.}
\end{frame}
