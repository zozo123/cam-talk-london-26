\begin{frame}[t]{If 50 of 100 tasks reach a checkpoint and 40 finish, overall success is 40\%.\ev{\tagA}}

\lead{From the checkpoint: $40/50=80\%$. \qquad From the start: $40/100=40\%$.}
\tagline{Both rates are useful. State which starting population you are measuring.}
\sources{Illustrative counts: $P(\mathrm{success})=P(\mathrm{checkpoint})\,P(\mathrm{success}\mid\mathrm{checkpoint})=0.5\times0.8$.}

\qadetail{The values .5 and .8 are illustrative, not Go-Explore or factory measurements. Illustrative probabilities, not measured. A viable reached state contains information about the prefix; continuation success alone does not estimate how often it is reached or validate every earlier decision. Go-Explore explicitly separates return-to-state and exploration. A success rate at a selected checkpoint differs from a task-start success rate.}
\note{[0:45] Start with a hundred tasks. Fifty reach the useful checkpoint; forty of those finish. From the checkpoint, success is forty out of fifty, or eighty percent. From the original task start, it is forty out of a hundred, or forty percent. Both rates answer useful but different questions. A checkpoint may make the remaining exploration easier. We should report that continuation rate alongside how often the checkpoint was reached. Otherwise a selected starting population can make the full process look more successful than it was.}
\end{frame}
