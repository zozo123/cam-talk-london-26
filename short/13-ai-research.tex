Five minutes is wall-clock training time; startup and compilation are outside that budget, and crashes can end early. Sequential Git keep/revert is the actual mechanism; forked parallel continuations are a possible extension, not what produced this result. Repeated adaptive selection on one validation set warrants a separate held-out check before claiming generalization. AlphaEvolve: DeepMind reports that the discovered Borg scheduling heuristic recovered an average 0.7\% of Google's worldwide compute resources, in production for over a year at announcement. \note{[1:00] Karpathy's autoresearch starts every attempt from the current best commit. The commit hash names that exact state. The evaluator stays fixed. The agent edits the training script and trains for five minutes. It reads validation bits per byte, where lower is better. Then it keeps or reverts. One reported session made 126 attempts, one at a time, and kept twenty-three. The score fell 2.8 percent. Starting from the best commit is compute once. Only the fixed evaluator promotes a change, and that is publish once. AlphaEvolve extends this loop to a population of programs. One scheduling heuristic it found recovered 0.7 percent of Google's compute, on average. Third example: software engineering.}
