\claim{Shared errors count for less.}
\vspace{.3cm}
{\centering\fontsize{12}{15}\selectfont{\color{Gold}\bfseries Proposed:}\enspace covariance-aware weights; refuse unsupported precision claims.\par}
\sources{Illustrative: equal noise, common correlation; precision of the mean, not a count of units. \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09689v4}{arXiv:2607.09689v4}.}

\qadetail{The 100-estimate example assumes equal marginal noise sigma and a common pairwise correlation, with the signal held fixed: Var = sigma^2[rho+(1-rho)/N], so noise is 0.10 sigma independent versus 0.33 sigma at rho=0.1, and the signal-to-noise gain is 10x versus 3.03x. N_eff = N/(1+(N-1)rho) is variance-equivalent precision for a mean, not a count of independent units, not a probability of correctness, and it does not remove shared bias. For N=9, effective counts are 9 at rho=0, 5 at rho=.1, 1.8 at rho=.5. Candidate search needs a different joint quantity (the all-wrong probability). The built Gaussian/Wald reference reducer pools under an independence/common-target model; lineage is transported but not yet consumed, and it does not abstain. Proposed: covariance-aware weights w = Sigma^-1 1 / (1^T Sigma^-1 1) (best linear unbiased under known positive-definite covariance; Valassi and Chierici 2014), with covariance from justified calibration rather than worker declarations; weights can be negative and common bias does not average away. Also proposed: abstention when dependence is unknown, trusted evidence identity and controller-derived information. Source: Eliaz, arXiv:2607.09689v4.}
\note{[0:50] Take a hundred equally noisy estimates whose errors have pairwise correlation point one. They might share a parent state, a seed or a test fixture. Their average reduces noise only as much as about nine independent estimates, not a hundred. Shared errors count for less, and a shared bias does not average away at all. Our reducer still assumes independence. Proposed: weights that use the error covariance, and refusing a precision claim when the dependence is unknown. Worse than a shared error is a worker that overstates its own precision.}
